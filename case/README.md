# hadistory — enclosure for the Pico version

A 3D printed case, portrait, with the Inky Impression 5.7" as the face and the electronics in the back shell: back shell, bezel, a cap over the HAT wiring, four push keys, a story dial thumbwheel and label plates (keys, ON / OFF, list of the stories). Design inspired by the [YouMakeTech Pico-GB](https://www.youmaketech.com/pico-gb-gameboy-emulator-handheld-for-raspberry-pi-pico/) bottom cover: every part sits in a moulded pocket or on standoffs, and all the wiring is Dupont jumpers.

Two styles in the same model, `style` at the top of the file. **`bump`** (v10, the default): 26 mm body, 10 mm corners, chamfered back edge, rounded bezel edge, and a pillow-shaped cap on the back over the HAT Dupont block, the only place that needs the depth. **`box`** (v7): the rectangular shell, 33 mm deep everywhere, validated by the test prints. The insides are the same. The `box` is the old design: it stays in the model, but its STL files are frozen as they were on 2026-11-06 in `stl/old_design/box/` and are no longer exported with each iteration.

## Files

| File | Content |
|---|---|
| `hadistory_case.scad` | Parametric model (OpenSCAD). All dimensions are in the parameter block at the top; `style` picks `bump` or `box`. |
| `stl/front.stl` | Bezel, exported face up (rounded edge on top) |
| `stl/cap.stl` | Cap over the HAT Dupont block, exported flange down, dome up; glued inside |
| `stl/key.stl` | Push key, print 4 |
| `stl/wheel.stl` | Story dial thumbwheel, print outer face down: numbers 24 degrees apart, for the 30-detent KY-040 at hand (two detents per number, firmware default) and for the AS5600 |
| `stl/swbar.stl` | Bar screwed on the post behind the switch, holds it down; print flat |
| `stl/ky040/back.stl` | Back shell for the incremental encoder (KY-040, `dial_sensor="ky040"`, the default), print floor down |
| `stl/as5600/back.stl`, `axle.stl`, `pocket.stl` | Back shell and the two extra parts for the magnetic sensor (`dial_sensor="as5600"`, v11b, not printed yet): the shell with the bearing boss, the two ribs the board is screwed on and the slot in the floor; the axle (print flange down); the pocket that closes the slot (print flange down) |
| `stl/plates/plate.stl` | Label plate for the keys, glued on the top edge of the case, print flat text up |
| `stl/plates/swplate_on_left.stl`, `swplate_on_right.stl` | ON / OFF plate for the switch, glued on the left edge over the switch opening, above the knob; print the one matching the wiring |
| `stl/plates/stories.stl` | List of the twelve stories with their numbers, glued on the back above the cap, print flat text up |
| `stl/old_design/box/` | The `box` style (v7 outline with the v10d insides, KY-040), as exported on 2026-11-06 and left as it is: back, front, key, wheel, swbar and the three plates |

A print is everything in `stl/` itself, one of the two dial folders, and the plates wanted.

Pictures: `img/bump/`, `img/box/` (old design), and the plates and concepts in `img/`.

Regenerate the STL files after a change:

```bash
cd case
for p in front cap key wheel swbar; do openscad -D "part=\"$p\"" -o stl/$p.stl hadistory_case.scad; done
openscad -D 'part="back"' -o stl/ky040/back.stl hadistory_case.scad
for p in back axle pocket; do openscad -D 'dial_sensor="as5600"' -D "part=\"$p\"" -o stl/as5600/$p.stl hadistory_case.scad; done
for p in plate stories; do openscad -D "part=\"$p\"" -o stl/plates/$p.stl hadistory_case.scad; done
openscad -D 'part="swplate"' -D 'sw_on_left=true'  -o stl/plates/swplate_on_left.stl  hadistory_case.scad
openscad -D 'part="swplate"' -D 'sw_on_left=false' -o stl/plates/swplate_on_right.stl hadistory_case.scad
```

The `box` style still renders (`-D 'style="box"'` on any of these lines), for whoever wants it back; nothing writes into `stl/old_design/`.

Other values of `part`: `assembly` (shell, cap, HAT and parts preview, bezel transparent), `closed` (all parts opaque, for pictures), `section` (assembly cut at `section_x`, keeping a slab `section_w` thick, to check the clearances: 85.6 goes through the HAT header and the cap, 50 through the Pico; `section_x=2 section_w=18` keeps the left wall with the switch and the encoder, to look at from inside; `section_y` and `section_yw` bound the slab along Y as well: `section_x=-20 section_w=70 section_y=76 section_yw=0.4` is a slice through the dial axis).

OpenSCAD on macOS: `brew install --cask openscad@snapshot` (the stable cask is disabled). The `.scad` file also opens in FreeCAD.

## How it goes together

The device is seen from the front in the model: X right, Y up, display towards the viewer. The Pimoroni drawing of the HAT is a front view with the components seen through (a mirrored version was tried in v2 and put the keys and the flex relief in the wrong place). Turned so that the buttons are on top: the 40-pin header and its Dupont block hang on the **right**, the panel flex is on the **left** edge, the four side buttons protrude from the **top** edge.

- **Depth** (`bump`): 26.6 mm in all, set by the Pico: floor 2, standoffs 8, Pico with its right-angle pins 8, then `hat_back_room` (3 mm, to measure: the clearance for the components on the back of the HAT PCB over the Pico, the header itself is elsewhere; the 4 mm module near the HAT's lower left corner, `hat_mod`, is over nothing but the switch wires), the PCB 2.8 plus the 0.8 drop, bezel 2. The Dupont housings on the HAT header, 18 mm below the PCB, end 1 mm above the floor; the cap gives them 6 mm more plus the floor thickness, for plugging. In `box`, 33 mm: the housings plus 10 mm of slack everywhere.
- **Outline** (`bump`): 10 mm plan radius, 3 mm chamfer at 45 degrees on the back edge (prints floor down as is, where a fillet would not), and a 2 mm bead at 45 degrees inside along the floor-wall junction so that 2.1 mm of material stays behind the chamfer. The corner bosses move 6 mm in from the faces (3.2 in `box`), and the top margin grows to 7 mm so that the top bosses clear the PCB corners.
- **Cap** (`bump`): a 38 x 67 mm pillow, 6 mm proud of the back, 12 mm plan radius, sides leaning in at 45 degrees and a 3 mm rounded top edge, centred on the HAT header strip (X 83 to 88, Y 45 to 96). It goes in from inside through a hole in the floor (0.2 mm clearance), its 3 mm flange sits on the inner face of the floor and is glued there before the HAT goes in; the flange also makes it captive. Walls 2 mm, the inside closes at 45 degrees so that it prints flange down without support. Its footprint stops short of the battery rib above and leaves the switch cradle clear below.
- **Back shell**: walls 2 mm, floor 2 mm. The HAT rests on eight small pads, 8 mm wide, reaching 1.6 mm under the PCB edge, 3.6 mm below the rim (`shelf_drop` 0.8 mm: 1 mm on v3 was too much, and with 0.4 the v7 glass stood 0.6 mm proud on the right side when the bezel was screwed); each pad hangs from the wall on a 45 degree gusset and carries a short locating lip outside the PCB: 0.4 mm on the long sides (`clear`, 0.8 mm on v3 left too much play), 0.1 mm on the top and bottom edges (`clear_y`: at 0.4 the v5 still had 0.6 mm of play along the PCB length). Two pads per long side, two on the bottom edge, two on the top edge between the keys, clear of the protruding caps (v4 had none on top: the PCB could ride up to the key guides, a millimetre or more off the bottom lips, and shifted at each key press). Everything else in the margins stays open: screwdriver access to the Pico, wires everywhere. Margins 4.5 mm on the sides, 6 mm on top, 12 mm chin.
- **Front**: a 2 mm plate with the window, four countersunk M3 screws into the corner bosses, and the conical hole of the sphere LED. It covers the notches of the keys and the switch opening at the rim, and holds the HAT down on its pads. In `bump` its outer edge is rounded (1.6 mm) and it prints face up, the countersinks and the LED cone being 45 degree features; `box` prints it face down.
- **Keys**: the HAT switches are on the back face of the PCB, at 19.8, 41.3, 62.7 and 84.2 mm from the flex edge (`btn_from_flex`); their caps protrude 1.5 mm from the top edge, below the PCB underside (v3 had the keys at the glass level, about 4 mm too high). Four guide blocks in the top margin, set back 2.4 mm from the PCB edge to clear the caps and hanging from the wall on a 45 degree gusset like the pads (no print support), carry a notch from 4.4 mm below the PCB underside up to the rim (`btn_cap_drop`), and a step for the 12 mm head of the key. The key is 7.6 mm tall and fills the notch, so it cannot ride up and reaches the cap wherever it sits in that range; its shaft stands 3 mm proud of the wall (`key_proud`, 1 mm on v4 was too short to find by feel). A key is dropped in from above before the HAT and is captive once the HAT and bezel are on. Its shaft and head are 0.6 mm narrower than their notch (`key_play`, 0.8 up to v6 felt loose with the 3 mm shaft outside), the head has 0.2 mm of play along the travel in its step (`key_head_play`) and the nub stops 0.3 mm short of the cap with the key fully out (`key_gap`): 0.5 mm of free travel before the click, where the v6 key had 1.1 mm. The cap protrusion (`btn_protrusion`, 1.5 mm) is still an estimate: any error on it adds to or subtracts from that free travel. The shell notches did not change: a new key fits a v6 shell.
- **Label plate**: a 92 x 8 x 1.2 mm plate glued on the outer face of the top wall, 1 mm behind the key notches, centred on the four keys. One word per key with a small pointer towards the key: left to right as seen from the front, BACK, MODE, RESET, NEXT (`plate_labels`; those are keys D, C, B, A, the firmware functions are in `pico/README.md`). Read with the display facing you and the top edge tilted towards you, or with the device lying display up and seen from beyond its top edge: the back of the case is up either way. Text and pointers stand 0.6 mm proud (`plate_relief`, a negative value engraves them instead); glue with cyanoacrylate or a dot of hot glue.
- **ON / OFF plate**: a 30 x 15.6 x 1.2 mm plate of the same construction, glued on the left wall over the switch opening, from 1 mm above the knob's travel slot up to the rim, ON on one side of the knob and OFF on the other (the words are along its lower edge, the rest of the plate hides the opening the switch went in through). Which side is on depends on how the switch was wired, so there are two variants (`sw_on_left`): `swplate_on_left.stl` and `swplate_on_right.stl`, pick the one that matches. It reads with the device face up. Glued last, after the switch check: it is what keeps the switch from riding up.
- **List of the stories**: a 101.6 x 36.4 x 1.2 mm plate of the same construction, glued on the back above the cap, over the battery: the one stretch of the back that is flat across the whole width and clear of the cap, of the TP4056 window (1.9 mm below the plate) and of the AS5600 pocket. Lower edge 3.4 mm above the cap (`story_y0`), centred across the case; it reads with the device turned over, top edge up. Twelve lines in two columns of six, the number of the wheel then a short title (`story_titles`, in the order of `stories/`; the titles of stories 1 to 7, which have none in their text, and the shortened "Le prince aux pois" are proposals to edit). Text 3.2 mm (`story_sz`), lines 5.6 mm apart: a title may be about 18 characters in the first column and 20 in the second (`story_col_w`: the width of a column is the number, a gap and its longest title; widen one and the plate widens; the back is flat over 107 mm). A single column is possible, `-D story_cols=1 -D 'story_col_w=[50.8]'`, 54.8 x 70 mm: it fits beside the cap with the KY-040, not with the AS5600 pocket.
- **Switch** (v10): SS12F15 on the left wall, Y 21.5, lying on the floor with its knob through the wall, pins pointing inwards. Why not at the rim as before: the chin gave 12 mm between the wall and the HAT PCB, the side margins give 4.5, and the body is 6 mm deep, so at the rim it would sit under the PCB; 11 mm lower nothing is above it. The v9 seat (switch pushed in from inside knob first, a keeper plate behind it) did not hold it, so v10 goes back to the v7 cradle: **the switch drops in from the front**, through an opening of the wall 8 mm wide from the knob up to the rim (`sw_slot_l`), its two 0.5 mm ears sliding down between the wall and a 1.6 mm lip 0.4 mm off them at each end (`sw_lip_t`, 10 mm tall), the body on a 1.2 mm pad (`sw_pad_t`) so that the ears float. Along the wall the ears end against an end block each side, 0.2 mm of play each; in and out they are held by the lips; up, by a **bar** (`swbar`, 1.6 mm, flat) screwed with an M3 self-tapping screw on a 5 x 5 mm post standing 0.2 mm behind the body, 6.8 mm up the wall from the switch centre (clear of the pins' housings), level with the body top: the bar overhangs the body's upper end by 3.7 x 4.8 mm, 0.1 mm above it (the first v10 print relied on the ON / OFF plate alone, which left the switch free to lift until the plate was glued); the plate still covers the opening and the bezel its top end. No glue on the switch, no screw; the pins and their housings are free behind the body, and the lower HAT pad moved up to Y 32 to 40 so that nothing hangs over the switch on its way in. Ear thickness and height are parameters (`sw_ear_t`, `sw_ear_h`), to check on the part.
- **Story dial** (v10): a KY-040 module standing behind the left wall, encoder towards the wall, PCB along the wall (26.1 x 18.6 mm, `enc_pcb_l`, `enc_pcb_w`) from Y 59 to 85.1, axis at Y 76, the middle of the edge. Where exactly is set by the HAT: a 26 x 10 mm module with a connector stands 4 mm proud of its back near the lower left corner (`hat_mod`: X 10.9 to 20.9, Y 18.4 to 44.4, drawn in the previews), more than the 3 mm of `hat_back_room`, and nothing of the dial may run under it: not the encoder PCB, 1.2 mm under the HAT, nor the five Dupont housings on the module's right-angle header, which lie along the PCB 14 mm below its lower end (Y 45 to 59, 0.6 mm above the module). The header is at the **lower** end, pins towards the bottom edge (`enc_pins_up` false), because that is what gives the wheel its size: the module's two mounting holes are both along one long edge (7.8 mm from the axis, 5.5 and 8.5 mm either side of it along the board, `enc_hole_dw`, `enc_hole_dl`; v9 had them across the board, hence a print with the holes off the posts), with the pins down that edge is the floor edge, the holes sit 2.6 mm above it (`enc_hole_edge`, to measure), the axis 7.8 higher, 11.6 mm above the back face, and the wheel is 21 mm, bottom 1.1 mm above the back, the size found right for the detents on the print (pins up would put the holes on the HAT side, the axis at 9.4 and the wheel at 18.4 at most: that was v10b, with the dial lower down). The left edge gets a notch, 4.5 mm deep and 27 mm long (Y 62.5 to 89.5, 2 mm corner radius), through the shell and the bezel; the 2 mm wall behind it ends at X 6.5, just outside the HAT PCB, and carries a 1.6 mm stop pin outside; it stands in the span of the panel flex (Y 63.8 to 89.8), so above Z 16 it is thinned to 1.2 mm, like the top of the boss next to it, leaving the fold 1.2 mm beyond the PCB edge. The encoder's bushing is 12 mm long (`enc_bush_h`, v9 assumed 7 and the wheel could not reach its stop) and the body 4 mm (`enc_body_h`): a 12 mm wide boss rises from the floor behind the notch wall and takes the length the wall and the nut do not, 6.6 mm, the body sits on its face and the bushing hole runs through wall and boss as a teardrop (it prints lying); outside, 3.4 mm of bushing inside the wheel's bore (`enc_bush_out`). Two 5 mm ribs from the floor, from X 7.5 (clear of the flex fold) to the PCB under the holes, take M3 screws from behind. **No nut** (`wheel_nut` false): the body against the boss, the bushing in its 8.6 mm long hole and the screw hold the encoder; a pull on the wheel presses the body on the boss, a push goes to the screw. The PCB stands in a 0.8 mm recess of the floor (`enc_floor_recess`), 1.2 mm under the HAT; the recess is 0.8 mm wider than the PCB each side and 2 mm longer each end (`enc_recess_play`): the bushing and the screw set where the PCB is, the recess only has to take it (on the first v10 print, 0.4 and 0.5 mm were too tight and the PCB stood on the floor, askew). The thumbwheel is 12 mm thick: 4 mm inside the notch, over the bushing end (8 mm bore), 8 mm proud of the edge. Its shaft bore is a blind D, 6.1 mm with the flat 1.7 mm from the axis (4.75 across), the shape of the knob cap printed earlier for this encoder, so the wheel presses on without glue; the 12 mm shaft is **cut to 7.8 mm beyond the bushing end** (4.2 mm off, echoed by the model). The D fixes the wheel's angle on the shaft, and the detents are fixed on the shaft too, so where the 1 lands at the lower stop is a property of the encoder: `wheel_phase` turns the numbers and the groove about the flat, set from a print (angle between the 1 and the arrow at the stop detent, 12 degrees per detent): with 0 the v10c print had the arrow between the numbers at every detent, so 12, half a step. Numbers 1 to 12 are engraved on the rim every `wheel_step` degrees, one firmware step apart, upright for the reader, 1 on top at the lower stop; an arrow 5 x 4.8 mm engraved 0.8 mm into the bezel margin (`arrow_*`) points at the number in use. On the inner face of the wheel, a 1.6 mm deep arc groove from radius 5.2 to 9.2 (`stop_groove_r`) takes the 1.6 mm stop pin at radius 7.2, 1.2 mm of play a side: the encoder has play in its recess and the v10c groove, 2 mm wide, did not take the pin; 11 steps of travel plus the pin and 3 degrees of clearance at each end (`stop_clear`), its ends are the stops; it is a closed ring between the bushing bore and 1.3 mm of rim (0.4 chamfer), nothing of the mechanism shows. With `wheel_nut` true the M7 nut sits in a 12.4 mm recess of the wheel and the groove moves out to radius 7.6, open into that recess, with 0.4 mm of rim: that is how the first v10 wheel was, and why the nut was dropped. `wheel_step` depends on the encoder. The KY-040 at hand has 30 detents a turn and gives half a quadrature cycle per detent, so the firmware's default count (one step per full cycle) moves one number every two detents: 24 degrees per number, 264 degrees of travel, three quarters of the wheel (`wheel.stl`). On a 20-detent encoder two detents per number would need 396 degrees, so the firmware would be built with `DIAL_DETENTS_PER_STEP` 1 (a step at every detent) and the wheel printed at 18 degrees (198 degrees of travel; that wheel is no longer exported, `-D wheel_step=18` on the wheel's line gives it). Count the detents on one turn of the bare encoder to know which. The firmware saturates its count at both ends, see `pico/README.md`.
- **Story dial, AS5600 variant** (v11b, `dial_sensor = "as5600"`, drawn before the sensor arrived: every `as_` value is to be measured). The AS5600 is a magnetic angle sensor: a small diametric magnet turns 0.5 to 3 mm over the chip, on its axis, and the chip gives the absolute angle. There is no shaft any more, so the wheel gets one: a printed **axle** that copies the KY-040's bushing and D shaft (7 mm journal, 6 mm D), turns in the same 7.4 mm bore through the notch wall and a shorter boss (a 6 mm bearing) and takes the same `wheel.stl`, pressed on its D. Its inner end is a 12 x 3.2 mm flange: it keeps the axle in, and the 6 x 2.5 mm magnet is pressed into its face, flush. The breakout (23 x 23 mm, chip in the middle) stands across the axis 1.6 mm behind the magnet. It is taller than the 19.8 mm free under the HAT, which sets everything else: its top edge goes 0.6 mm under the HAT, so the axis comes down from 11.6 to 8.9 mm and the 21 mm wheel dips 1.6 mm under the back face (the notch is cut through the floor already), and its lower edge goes through a slot of the floor, 2.6 mm beyond the back face. **The board is screwed on the shell**, as the KY-040 is: two 5 mm ribs rise from the floor on the chip side, one each side of the boss (joined to it), from X 7.5 (clear of the flex fold) to X 15; each ends in a head that comes out to the board at 45 degrees and takes the board's corner, with a 2.5 mm pilot hole 5.6 mm deep (`as_hole_pilot`, `as_pilot_l`) facing one of the board's two upper holes (`as_hole_edge`), for an M3 x 6 self-tapping screw driven from behind the board. The ribs' tops fall at 45 degrees towards the wall, from the board's top edge down to 16 mm (`as_rib_top0`): nothing of the dial stood higher than 17.6 mm there before, and the back of the HAT is not measured in that corner. Under the floor, a separate **pocket** closes the slot around the board's lower edge, 4 mm proud of the back (the cap is 6): it drops in from inside before the board, its flange on the floor on three sides, and the board's edge, 0.2 mm above its bottom (`as_pocket_gap`), then keeps it there, so nothing is glued (v11 had the board on a cradle glued in the floor: no screw into the shell, and the board's position depended on the glue joint). Assembly: magnet into the flange (press it on a flat surface), axle through the bore from inside, wheel on the D from outside; pocket down beside the ribs' heads, then slid under them into the slot; board down along the heads into the pocket, chip towards the wall, two screws. The board is square with a hole in each corner, so it can be turned to put its pads on the side that suits the wires; solder them on the back: on the chip side the board lies against the heads at its two upper corners and has only 0.6 mm in the pocket. The stop pin and the groove of the wheel still limit the travel; the detents and the push button are gone (see `pico/README.md`: the angle is read, the 1 is calibrated once). In `box` the axis stays at 11.6 and the pocket is 1.3 mm proud. Steel screws near the magnet bias the reading a little, which the calibration absorbs as long as they do not move.
- **Panel flex**: on the left edge, between the two left pads; nothing touches the PCB edge there.
- Outer size 113 x 148 x 33 mm.

| From | To |
|---|---|
| Battery + / - | TP4056 B+ / B- |
| Pico VBUS (pin 40) | TP4056 IN+ : the Pico's USB charges the battery |
| TP4056 IN- / B- | Pico GND |
| TP4056 B+ | Slide switch, centre pin |
| Slide switch, side pin | Anode of a 1 A Schottky diode (1N5817, 1N5819, SS14; a 1N4007 also works with a larger drop). Not a 1N5711, it is a 15 mA signal diode. |
| Diode cathode | Pico VSYS (pin 39) |

The diode keeps the USB 5V from reaching the battery through the Pico when the USB is plugged with the switch on. A TP4056 module without B+/B- protection relies on the protection circuit of the cell itself (most 103450 pouch cells have one).

Signal wiring: see the wiring tables in `pico/README.md` (display on GP17 to GP22, buttons on GP2 to GP5, LED on GP8), with the HAT's 3V3 and GND.

## Printing

PLA or PETG, 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.2 mm layers, 3 walls, no supports: every overhang in the shell (pads, key guides, switch pad) sits on a 45 degree gusset, the back chamfer and the inner bead are at 45 degrees, the cap prints flange down with its dome narrowing upwards and a 45 degree ceiling inside, the `bump` bezel prints face up. The back shell always prints floor down; a rounded back would have needed supports or the shell printed rim down, which would have left the Pico standoffs and the ribs in the air, hence the chamfer and the separate cap. The model keeps heights on multiples of 0.2 mm and thin features on multiples of 0.4 mm: when adjusting a parameter, move by at least one layer (0.2 mm) or one line (0.4 mm), smaller changes are not reproduced. Printed holes come out smaller than modelled: the M3 pilot holes are 2.6 mm, the Pico M2 holes 2.0 mm.

Label plates (keys, ON / OFF, list of the stories): flat, text up, 0.2 mm layers (0.1 mm looks better on the letters). The strokes of the 4 mm words are about 0.6 mm wide, those of the list of the stories (3.2 mm) about 0.45 mm, one line of the nozzle, so enable the slicer's thin wall / gap fill option (PrusaSlicer "Detect thin walls", Cura "Print thin walls"), or print at 0.4 mm line width with a single perimeter for the top 0.6 mm. For two colours, change the filament at 1.2 mm, where the text starts; or set `plate_relief` to -0.6 and fill the engraved letters with paint.

## Print checklist

Version 10 (encoder seat and switch seat redone after the v9 print: the module's holes were across the posts, its 12 mm bushing left the wheel short of its stop, and the switch did not hold; v9 brought the story dial and the switch to the left edge and the green sphere to the right of the chin, on top of v8: `bump` outline, depth and cap, TP4056 glued components down with an LED window in the floor; same insides in both styles) after test prints of versions 1 to 6 of the box (v4: keys, LED, switch and Pico all good, the HAT had play towards the chin; v5: 0.6 mm of play left along the PCB length, keys too short to find by feel; v6: label plate, keys loose with 1.1 mm of free travel, switch rocking in its grooves; v7: glass and switch 0.6 mm proud on the right side when screwing the bezel, hence `shelf_drop` 0.8 and `sw_drop` 0.4). To check with the parts placed, before wiring:

1. HAT: drops onto the eight pads with little play (`clear`) in both directions, glass 0.8 mm below the rim (`shelf_drop`), buttons free above the top margin, flex free on the left edge.

3. Pico: screwed on its standoffs, housings on the pins clear of the floor, USB centred in its opening (`pico_standoff`, `pico_pos()`).
4. Dupont housings on the HAT header: nothing touches the floor with the HAT in place (`dupont_slack`).
5. Switch: dropped in from the front through the wall opening, ears sliding behind the two lips down to the pad (`sw_ear_len`, `sw_ear_t`, `sw_ear_h`), ends between the two end blocks with little play; the knob slides in the lowest 3.4 mm of the opening and the switch does not rock or back off when the knob is pushed in; the bar screwed on its post (`sw_post_dy`) sits on the body's upper end and stops it from lifting; the ON / OFF plate, held over the opening, leaves the knob free; glue the plate only after this check.
6. Screws: M3 bite in the bosses without splitting them.
7. TP4056: components down along the left wall, USB end up, it sits level on its USB shell and the rib under its B+/B- end (`tp_usb_h`), between the lip, the end stop and the left wall (`tp_l`, `tp_w`, `tp_clear`); its LEDs show through the floor window from the back (`tp_led_x`, `tp_win`); glue it only after this check, with the wires already soldered on the bare side.
8. Story dial: the encoder has 30 detents a turn (counted), `wheel.stl` is numbered every two detents; the KY-040 PCB stands in its floor recess, pins towards the bottom edge, their housings clear of the HAT's back module (`hat_mod`), the bushing through the wall and the boss with the body flat on the boss face, its two holes on the ribs (`enc_hole_dl`, `enc_hole_dw`, `enc_hole_edge`, `enc_cx`: measure them on the module), screwed from behind, the bushing end 3.4 mm out of the wall, no nut (`enc_bush_h`, `enc_bush_out`, `enc_body_h`); the PCB must not touch the HAT; the housings on the header run down the wall between the PCB and the HAT's module; shaft cut to the echoed length (7.8 mm beyond the bushing end); the wheel pressed on its D turns freely in the notch and reaches the stop pin, its groove over the pin with room on both sides (v10d: 4 mm wide, pin at r 7.2, the back's pin moved 1 mm out with it), 22 detents (11 numbers) between the stops; at the lower stop the 1 is under the arrow, else measure the offset, set `wheel_phase`, reprint the wheel. Held against the lower stop the wheel must sit a little past the 1's detent and spring back up to it when let go, never stop short of it: the firmware forgives the first but not the second (`stop_clear`, and the play of the wheel on the D flat, `wheel_bore_flat`, measured on the shaft; a wheel that turns a few degrees on its shaft moves the stops with it).
9. Story dial, AS5600 variant (when the sensor is there): measure the board (`as_pcb`, `as_pcb_t`), its holes (`as_hole_edge`, and their diameter for `as_hole_pilot`), the chip height (`as_chip_h`) and the magnet (`as_magnet_d`, `as_magnet_h`), and look for anything on the back of the HAT above the ribs (X 7.5 to 17, Y 64.5 to 69.5 and 82.5 to 87.5, within 5 mm of the PCB: `as_rib_top0`), before printing anything. Then: the magnet is a firm press fit in the flange, flush; the axle turns freely in the bore with little wobble and about 0.8 mm of end play; the wheel holds on the D; the pocket goes down beside the heads of the ribs, slides under them and drops into the slot, its flange flat on the floor, clear of the TP4056 (0.5 mm); the board goes down along the heads, its two upper holes on the pilot holes, its top edge clear of the HAT (`as_top_gap`), its lower edge just above the bottom of the pocket, which no longer lifts; the screws bite without splitting the heads; the console says "magnet detected" with neither "too weak" nor "too strong" (`as_gap`); the wheel does not rub on a table with the device on its back.

Mode indication (decided 2026-10-21): the firmware draws a robot pictogram in the top-left corner of every AI page, where the story mode draws its page counter, so that the panel itself tells the mode, even asleep or off (`img/robot_badge.png`); the status LED keeps the mode bursts of the Pi version. - **Story dial**: a KY-040 encoder standing behind the left wall, its threaded bushing through the wall. Outside, a thumbwheel half sunk in a notch of the edge, the numbers 1 to 12 upright on its rim one firmware step apart (24 degrees: two detents of the 30-detent encoder), an arrow on the bezel pointing at the one in use. A pin in an arc groove limits the travel to 11 steps, 22 detents: the end stops are the absolute reference of an otherwise incremental encoder, the firmware saturates its index at 1 and 12. In the functional model the wheel ended up 21 x 12 mm (the encoder PCB sets the axis height, the bushing and nut need the thickness), at Y 47 to stay clear of the HAT pad below and the panel flex above. Selection is blind by design (no scrolling on a 30 s panel) and nothing counts during the deep sleep, so the ritual is: wheel down to the stop, press the wheel (RESET, re-arms the count at 1), turn up to the number, GO.
- **Switch** moved to the left edge under the wheel, with its ON / OFF plate; the bottom edge keeps only the Pico USB. In the functional model it lies on the floor: at the rim, as before, its 6 mm body would have met the HAT PCB, the left margin being 4.5 mm where the chin was 12.
- **Second sphere** at the right end of the chin (X 95), where the switch was: no conflict with the Dupont housings, no button wire moves. White = activity, green = AI mode.
- Firmware side: done behind the `STORY_DIAL` build option (`pico/README.md`), deep sleep kept as it is: the wheel is counted awake only, hence the re-arm by RESET. Counting asleep (dormant mode) stays a possible later step.

**v10 (2026-10-23)**, from the first v9 print: the KY-040 was modelled from a guess, the print showed its holes are both along one long edge of the board (the datasheet picture and the user's own face plate agree: 7.8 mm from the axis, 5.5 and 8.5 mm along the board), its bushing is 12 mm long, not 7, and its header is a right-angle one. So the module turned a quarter turn, the posts became ribs on the floor, a boss behind the notch wall takes the extra bushing length. Then the HAT turned out to carry a 4 mm module on its back near the lower left corner, right where the encoder stood: the dial first moved up to Y 54.6 with the pins towards the top edge to keep the housings clear of it, which put the holes on the HAT side, the axis at 9.4 mm and the wheel at 18.4 mm; the user wanted the 21 mm wheel back, so the dial went up to Y 76 with the pins down again, the housings now ending 0.6 mm above the module, the TP4056 above the notch wall at Y 92, and the nut was dropped so that the stop groove could be a closed ring again. The switch seat went back to the v7 idea, a cradle the switch drops into from the front, with the wall opened up to the rim and the ON / OFF plate covering the opening.

**v11 (2026-11-05), AS5600 variant, on paper**: the incremental encoder needs its ritual (stop, RESET, count) and loses its place whenever the wheel turns while the board sleeps. An AS5600 reads the wheel's absolute angle instead. The cost is mechanical: no shaft, no detents, no push button, and a 23 mm board where 19.8 mm are free. Options weighed: the board flat on the floor with a horizontal disc as a wheel (numbers readable from the edge only, a 33 mm disc among the switch, the flex and the TP4056: a new layout); the board in the 4.9 mm margin beside the HAT (no room for a bearing or for the notch); the board standing as the KY-040 did, with the axis lowered and its lower edge in a pocket through the floor. The last one keeps the wheel, the notch, the arrow, the stop and the front shell, and adds two small parts: kept, behind `dial_sensor`. **v11b (2026-11-06)**: the board was screwed on a cradle that was itself only glued in the floor; it is now screwed on two ribs of the shell, and what is left of the cradle is a pocket closing the floor, held by the board.
